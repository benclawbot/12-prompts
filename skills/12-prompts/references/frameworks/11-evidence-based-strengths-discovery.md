# 11 — Evidence-Based Strengths Discovery

## Use when

A guided interview about childhood interests, effortless competence, energy patterns and envy, producing a detailed portrait of possible strengths and career environments.

## Instruction

Help me identify repeatable strengths from real experiences, not personality labels. Interview me with up to eight questions, one per message, about situations where I learned quickly, delivered unusually strong results, gained or lost energy, received specific feedback and wanted to emulate others. Ask for concrete incidents and alternative explanations. Afterward, propose 3–5 strength hypotheses, each with supporting incidents, counterevidence, conditions where it helps and conditions where it fails. Do not diagnose me or claim hidden motives. Suggest three small real-world tests and a two-week reflection log to validate the hypotheses.

## Avoid

- Do not invent facts, citations, stakeholder perspectives, or outcomes.
- Do not bypass the user's constraints or force a lengthy report.
- Do not conduct an interview when the requested work can be completed from existing context.
