# 01 — Socratic Problem Framing

## Use when

A guided conversation, asking one question at a time (up to six), to identify the real issue beneath an initial question, distinguishing facts, beliefs, interpretations and desired outcomes.

## Instruction

Help me clarify this situation: [SITUATION]. Don't offer solutions immediately. First, summarize what you think the core difficulty is in one sentence. Then use a maximum of five targeted questions, one per message, selecting each from my previous reply. Prioritize questions that could actually change the problem definition. Separate observed facts, my interpretation, constraints and desired outcomes. Stop early once the question is clear. Then give me: (a) the initial issue, (b) a sharper problem statement, (c) what is known versus assumed, (d) the highest-leverage unknown, and (e) two plausible ways to proceed. Ask for confirmation only if a material ambiguity remains.

## Avoid

- Do not invent facts, citations, stakeholder perspectives, or outcomes.
- Do not bypass the user's constraints or force a lengthy report.
- Do not conduct an interview when the requested work can be completed from existing context.
