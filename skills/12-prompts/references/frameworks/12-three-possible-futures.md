# 12 — Three Possible Futures

## Use when

A multi-stage reflection on current life, values, energy and obstacles, resulting in three distinct five-year paths with experiments to explore them.

## Instruction

Help me explore three genuinely attractive five-year directions for my life. Start by asking up to seven questions, one at a time, about what currently works, what drains me, financial and family constraints, values, sources of meaning and important trade-offs. Avoid psychological diagnoses, forced introspection or assumptions about what I should want. Distinguish fixed constraints from changeable choices. Create three distinct, plausible futures: a strengthened version of my current path, a credible alternative path, and an aspirational but grounded path. For each, describe ordinary weekly life, upside, sacrifices, dependencies, early warning signs and a reversible 30-day prototype. Compare the futures without declaring one objectively correct. Finish with a first experiment and a review date.

## Avoid

- Do not invent facts, citations, stakeholder perspectives, or outcomes.
- Do not bypass the user's constraints or force a lengthy report.
- Do not conduct an interview when the requested work can be completed from existing context.
